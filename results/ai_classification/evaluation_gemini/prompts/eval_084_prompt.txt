# Part A — Shared classification instructions

## Categories

| Code | Category |
| ---- | -------- |

## Definitions, inclusion criteria and exclusions

### 0 — World
- **Definition:** political, military, diplomatic, social or natural events
  reported primarily as happenings in the world, including national politics,
  conflicts, elections (as political events), crime, disasters and
  humanitarian situations.
- **Include:** government action, wars and peace processes, elections as
  political events, crime and justice as events, natural disasters, refugee
  situations, international relations, and disease outbreaks or
  government/public-health responses **when the focus is the event itself or
  the social/political response to it**.
- **Exclude:** stories whose main focus is the economic or market impact of
  such events (→ Business), sporting events however international (→ Sports),
  or scientific/technical content however geopolitical the setting (→ Sci/Tech
  if science/technology is the focus).

### 1 — Sports
- **Definition:** competitive sport and its direct practice: matches, results,
  players, teams, leagues, tournaments, transfers and coaching.
- **Include:** game results and analysis, player moves, injuries in a sporting
  context, records, competitions.
- **Exclude:** the business side of sport when the focus is money rather than
  the sport itself (ownership sales, franchise valuations, broadcast-rights
  economics → Business); sporting-politics disputes whose focus is political
  (→ World).

### 2 — Business
- **Definition:** commercial and economic activity: companies, markets,
  finance, earnings, deals, prices of traded goods, employment and labor
  economics, consumer economy.
- **Include:** earnings and outlook, mergers and acquisitions, stock and bond
  markets, interest rates and central-bank decisions as economic policy, trade
  and tariffs as economic matters, layoffs and labor disputes as economic
  matters, energy and commodity prices when the focus is the market, and —
  when they are the main focus — economic indicators (growth, inflation,
  unemployment, consumer confidence), the housing and mortgage market, and
  the effects of interest-rate changes on consumers, companies and markets.
- **Exclude:** technology products or research whose focus is the technology
  itself (→ Sci/Tech); the sport itself even when large sums are mentioned
  (→ Sports); political events whose focus is political rather than economic
  (→ World).

### 3 — Sci/Tech
- **Definition:** natural science, research findings, medicine and health
  research, and technology: computers, internet, software, telecommunications,
  space, engineering.
- **Include:** research results and discoveries, space missions, medical
  research, new treatments and drug developments **when the focus is the
  science**, new products or services when the focus is the product or its
  capabilities, cybersecurity, scientific policy debates as science events.
- **Exclude:** company financial results or deals whose focus is money and
  markets (→ Business); political controversies whose focus is the politics
  (→ World); disease outbreaks and public-health measures whose focus is the
  event or the social/political response rather than the science (→ World).

## General decision rule — main focus

Read the **whole text** and classify by its **main focus**: the event the text
devotes the majority of its content to.

- The headline is **evidence**, but it does not automatically override the
  body. If headline and body point in different directions, weigh the full
  content.
- **Do not classify by isolated keywords, company names, place names or sport
  names alone.** A text mentioning a company is not automatically Business; a
  text mentioning a team is not automatically Sports.
- Mentions of money do not by themselves make a text Business; mentions of
  research do not by themselves make a text Sci/Tech. Ask what the text is
  *about*, not what it *mentions*.
- When two categories remain equally defensible after reading the whole text,
  use the **central event stated in the lead** (the opening sentence).
- If the text is still unresolved after that, choose the most defensible of
  the four categories (the best-supported interpretation). How confidence is
  recorded is described in Part B (human annotator only).

## Tie-breaking rules

Apply only when, after the general decision rule, two categories are genuinely
plausible. Decide by the following pair-specific rules.

### Business (2) vs Sci/Tech (3)
If the text is about a technology-related subject, ask whether the focus is
the **technology itself** (what it does, how it works, capabilities, research)
→ Sci/Tech, or the **money and market side** (earnings, stock moves, deal
value, market share, prices) → Business. A product *announcement* focused on
features → Sci/Tech; a company's *results* or *valuation* → Business.

Acquisitions, takeover bids and mergers are Business **when the main focus is
the transaction or its commercial consequences**, even if the companies
involved are technology companies; the venue of an announcement (a
conference, an event) is a setting, not the main focus. Product features,
capabilities or research remain Sci/Tech when that is the main focus. No
category has automatic priority: the main-focus rule decides.

### World (0) vs Business (2)
If an event has both a geopolitical and an economic face, classify by what the
text emphasizes: the conflict, diplomacy, political decisions or human impact
→ World; the market, trade, prices, growth or company consequences → Business.
Example of the split: negotiations ending a dispute are World; the dispute's
effect on import costs is Business.

### Sports (1) vs Business (2)
If money and sport both appear, classify by whether the text is about the
**sporting activity** (playing, results, competition, sporting implications)
→ Sports, or about the **commercial transaction or economics** (purchase price,
valuation, investment, broadcast rights as a deal) → Business. A record
transfer reported as sports news of a player moving → Sports; the same
transfer reported primarily as a valuation of the buying club's owners →
Business.

### Sporting disputes, published criticism and cybersecurity incidents
Published criticism and disputes between clubs, players, leagues or fans —
statements, open letters or articles attacking a rival, including when
published on a club's own website — are classified by their sporting subject
→ Sports. A cybersecurity incident is Sci/Tech **when the main focus is the
technical incident** (hacking, data breach, website defacement, outage caused
by an attacker); political or financial consequences of the incident may
justify another category under the main-focus rule. Read carefully: "attack"
in a headline can mean a published criticism or a technical incident; the
body decides. No automatic priority applies.

### Texts that fit no category well
Some texts — for example culture, tourism, books or lifestyle items — may fit
none of the four categories well; poor fit is a possible case, not a verified
general property of the dataset. In such a case:
choose the **most defensible** of the four categories under the definitions
and tie-break rules; the human annotator then records confidence as described
in Part B. Do not create a fifth category and do not automatically
default to World. **A fallback category ("other", "unclear") does not exist:
exactly one of the four labels is always required.**

   valuation."* → Business despite the sport: the focus is the ownership
   transaction and its price, not play on the field. (Sports vs Business
   rule.)
